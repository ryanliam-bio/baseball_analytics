It's all fun and games predicting award runouts halfway through the year, but auditing at the end of the day is inevitably an exercise in humility. All four MVP and Cy Young winners were unanimous, and nearly all of this year's drama came from NL ROY.

I actually started writing this the week prior to winners being officially announced, content to lean on Kalshi's 99% odds for each, before deciding to hold off on publishing due to this nagging doubt that I was on the same page as the voters. Honestly great call by the haters, as I whiffed horribly on my biggest disagreement with the market: I spent the last three months of the season telling anyone who'd listen that Sal Stewart was an easy fade.

## AL MVP

</div>

> Despite the considerable gap to 2nd in offensive value, Yordan Alvarez will likely not finish as the AL WAR leader.

Mostly correct! Bpro disagrees, but both **Kevin McGonigle** and **Bobby Witt Jr.** did wind up marginally ahead of **Yordan** in bWAR and fWAR respectively. Mandatory disclaimer aside that such small WAR differences should be considered negligible, BWJ's chances at claiming his first MVP award were likely dashed by his late July IL stint and surrounding offensive struggles, though his fastball timing did improve markedly from our [mid-summer check-in](https://ryanliam-bio.github.io/baseball_analytics/projects/awards-market-2026/#al-mvp).

> Ben Rice, Nick Kurtz, and Junior Caminero all profile as worse versions of Yordan, with >90th percentile wOBAs and bad defense.

Frankly this race turned out to be pretty boring. Yordan didn't miss more than a few games down the stretch and secured his spot as the top hitter in baseball this season, while a thumb injury ended **Kurtz**'s season in August, **Ben Rice** cooled off around the same time, and **Caminero** mostly held serve to earn himself a 2nd place finish. Quick hat tip to **Randy Arozarena**, who to my surprise posted an AL top 5 WAR figure: a career high 5.5 on the back of a 151 wRC+. He cut his strikeout rate >5% YoY and added about 45 points to every part of his triple slash. An 83rd percentile xwOBA in Seattle is no small feat.

Personally I'm thrilled that Yordan finally got his hardware after tough injury luck limited him over the last few years. This guy has been one of the most complete hitters in baseball since his debut and it's so fun to see him able to show that for nearly 700 PA this season. Congrats to Yordan, who entered the year with only a ~3% market chance to take his first MVP award.

## NL MVP

</div>

I can't really single out one quote to show where I missed here because I'm not sure any of it wound up correct. After our [mid-July writeup](https://ryanliam-bio.github.io/baseball_analytics/projects/awards-market-2026/#nl-mvp), **Shohei Ohtani** was barely above average offensively and did not pitch once. **James Wood** missed a month thanks to an oblique strain and wasn't nearly as effective after his return. **PCA**, to his credit, never found that cold streak I warned of and ultimately turned in one of the ~5 best position player seasons of the 21st century by fWAR (10.6).

> … it's really hard to maintain >10% walks with a 30th percentile chase rate and a 19th percentile whiff rate.

Well, you know what, he did just that. 11.8% walk rate, 23rd percentile chase, and 38th percentile whiff on the year. His less aggressive approach (59.5% -> 49.8% swing%) paired beautifully with the extra 2.4mph of bat speed this season, as he finished the #2 hitter in baseball by wRC+. Add in a league-best 26 OAA and I have nothing left to do but tip my cap. Pull the ball hard in the air and good things happen.

> Otto Lopez is suddenly projected to be a 5.5 win player.

Shoutout to **Otto**, who did wind up right around 6 WAR by both FanGraphs and Bref and went from a legitimate offensive minus to well above average. **Brice Turang**, **Alex Bregman**, **Gabriel Moreno**, and **Elly De La Cruz** similarly deserve flowers for landing around the 5.5 WAR mark.

## AL CY

</div>

> The stuff models like [Cam Schlittler's] curveball too, which he says he isn't throwing often simply because he hasn't needed it yet.

In hindsight, this one probably should've been an easy call. **Schlittler** was getting elite results without using his highly graded secondary- turns out that's a pretty easy switch to flip once opposing teams lock in on the fastballs a bit more. Post-ASB he improved across the board (K-BB%, ERA, FIP, SIERA, etc.) on the back of a doubled curveball usage (7% -> 14%). All told, Cam finished the season with the highest RA9-WAR in baseball, a 1.95 ERA, and a 25% K-BB%. Simply dominant.

> [Cease's] is still a profile that could blow up any given day with poor walk + homer timing, but the swing and miss is as dominant as you'll find in the American League.

Credit to **Dylan Cease** as he mostly avoided the blowups, despite the walk rate getting a bit worse in the second half. July and August were dominant, while September was brutal by underlying ERA estimators, even if it didn't fully show in the box score. 5.5 fWAR is a career high for Cease but he once again falls a bit short of CY recognition.

> I hope that the improved arsenal is enough to keep [Joe Ryan's] career-low HR/FB% down…

It was not. Working around a late August glute strain that cost him a few weeks, **Ryan** posted a 6.82 ERA the rest of the way. The HR/FB% jumped back up near league-worst and the strikeouts fell a bit. The combination of injury history and homer-prone arsenal probably caps his ceiling below CY-level going forward, but he still has the tools to be a top-20ish starter when healthy.

> Without an approach change to limit barrels, I don't think the top-notch strikeout/walk numbers will be enough to bring [deGrom] back from a 3.49 ERA into contention.

The strikeouts improved, the walks got worse, and a brutal .348 BABIP/60.5% LOB% combination buried any chance of competing for hardware this season. He did clear 30 starts and finished 6th in fWAR but that peak deGrom dominance isn't coming back as long as he's atop the wrong end of the Hard Hit% leaderboard.

> These Seattle pitchers and their splits are as unreliable as ever.

**Woo**'s splits got a little better, but he still wound up middle of the pack across the board in the second half. Both sides of **Gilbert**'s Home/Away ERA worsened as he was the only starter above 60 IP in the 2H with a worse HR/FB% than Joe Ryan. Super weird, not something I'd expect to repeat next year, but worth digging into more.

> It seemed that more regular changeup usage had unlocked a new strikeout upside to complement [Rasmussen's] low walk rate and lack of hard contact, looking like a legitimate CY contender for the first time in his 3-TJ addled career.

The two blowups going into the break were not signs of things to come, as **Drew** dominated the second half with a 1.86 ERA (even if he overperformed the underlying numbers a bit). He finished the season with 5.0 fWAR, a 2.67 ERA in 167 IP, and a 4th-place CY finish behind Parker Messick.

> Parker Messick, Nick Martinez, Sonny Gray, and Emerson Hancock make up one big group of guys I don't expect to hold onto their sparkling ERAs…

How wrong I was to include **Parker Messick** in this group. He improved top-to-bottom after the break and earned himself a finalist nod for both ROY and CY. The command is elite, which held his profile up while the others fell back to Earth.

Quick nod to the previously unmentioned **Gavin Williams** who had the best 2H K-BB% (31.5%) in baseball, but was undone by a bottom-10 HR/FB%. That's a bigger problem when opposing hitters are keeping the ball off the ground 64% of the time. Despite that, he doesn't allow much hard contact and should see a LOB% that lines up more with his elite strikeout rate than 2026's slightly-above-average mark of 76.7%. I doubt the market will price him generously, especially after collecting the most strikeouts in the playoffs, but I would love Gavin as a dark horse CY contender next season.

## NL CY

</div>

> If [Misiorowski] pitches true to his 175 IP projection this shouldn't be much of a contest.

Pop the champagne! This race was probably the one I had most correct top to bottom. **The Mis** did "regress" to a 2.12 ERA and 28% K-BB% post-ASB while coming up 1 out shy of the aforementioned 175-inning mark. Of course, he still put up the highest single-season strikeout rate in the modern era and wound up healthily clear of the field in RA9-WAR, so I didn't expect the vote tally to be particularly close. There's not much else to say except congratulations to (arguably) the nastiest starting pitcher of all time.

> [Cristopher Sanchez] is as good of a volume bet as ever and should have better batted ball luck going forward.

He did not. The volume was there but his BABIP held around .333, he went ~1.2% the wrong way in both strikeout and walk rate, and his LOB% subsequently dropped 4 points. All this led to a season-long 2.91 ERA on 207 innings pitched; still a stellar year, but ultimately just another silver medal for his efforts.

> Jesús Luzardo and Paul Skenes… would need all-time second halves to offset their ~3.5 ERAs.

**Luzardo** gave us the exact runout needed to put himself in CY contention and it simply didn't matter because of how dominant J-Mis was. He was the easiest fantasy buy-low call of the year based on the canyon between actual and expected results, but that doesn't make his NL-best second half 1.82 ERA any less impressive. **Paul** unfortunately went the opposite direction with a 4.23 ERA post-break as his average four-seam velocity continued to trend well below his career norm and every underlying number worsened. He never hit the IL, even amidst months of injury speculation, so the offseason kicks off with a hefty open question for the Pirates ace.

> Yamamoto has excelled at generating weak contact but the perpetual 6-man rotation limits his innings upside and the strikeouts aren't as gaudy as the names above him.

He ultimately posted 185 innings (T5 in NL) and overperformed his ERA estimators by ~3/4 of a run thanks to elite contact suppression. This guy rocks.

> Chris Sale and Zack Wheeler… both project to cap out around 170 innings and are running career best LOB% marks without career best strikeout rates to support them.

Both landed around 162 innings and **Wheeler** did backslide into a 4.17 ERA rest of season (still wildly impressive coming off TOS!), but this season was vintage **Chris Sale** through and through. He pushed his velocity to levels he hasn't hit since 2018, which drove a 2.09 ERA and a sparkling 31.4% K-BB% to match. Way to let ’em know the old man still has it.

## AL ROY

</div>

> Injury aside, this is probably just McGonigle's to lose.

I could write 3 pages about how fun Kevin McGonigle is. I'll let my fan side show through a bit and say he made me more excited for Tigers baseball (womp womp 2026) than any player in the last decade. The Oyster Analytics team staked their claim on McGonigle as a top 5 prospect back in September of 2024 and boy has he paid off that projection. Frankly, I don't think much *needs* to be said about his season because it largely speaks for itself. T5 in f/bWAR. 129 wRC+ driven by a truly elite plate approach. He can hang at SS defensively. This is such an awesome young player with room to quickly become a superstar with any power add.

> [Murakami] has the firepower to do so- it's just a matter of outrunning a 2nd percentile strikeout rate and poor defense.

After a great first couple weeks back from IL, **Murakami** struggled at the dish rest of season. He showed enough incredible flashes throughout his first year that there shouldn't be much long-term panic but that contact rate is always going to make for a volatile profile.

> Messick will be a down-ballot CY contender if he can hold current form (a big if, as always) and Tolle has shown flashes of a future ace.

Messick did better than down-ballot and **Tolle** finished with a 3.03 ERA, backed up by 21.7% K-BB% and 3.34 SIERA. It's just really hard to overcome a season like Kevin's, as evidenced by 28/30 first-place votes.

## NL ROY

</div>

For every award thus far, the result felt straightforward enough that we didn't need a full ballot breakdown. This year's NL Rookie of the Year race, however, made no sense to me. I could not have missed much worse on this over the last few months.

> [Wetherholt] projects to be… a 5 WAR player by end of season but the defensive carrying tool leaves the door open a little more than the immediate WAR read would suggest.

**Wetherholt** played through wrist tendinitis for about a month before hitting the IL in late August. That cratered his offensive value while **Sal Stewart** was on fire, which probably cemented this race as over in most voters' minds. I'd like to think that JJ would have walked it in had he stayed healthy, given he had a 117 wRC+ before the break and was one of the best defenders in baseball.

> Nolan McLean was a preseason favorite but had a brutal May before recovering pretty well over the last 6 weeks (see: rookie pitchers).

**Nolan** held a respectable 2.61 second-half ERA despite significantly worse underlying numbers. His 3.5 fWAR earned him 4th place in ROY voting.

> Carson Benge and A.J. Ewing both look like solid big-leaguers in the long run, just not quite explosive enough to close the gap in a timely manner.

**Carson Benge** was the only contender to approach Wetherholt's WAR numbers. While I'm hesitant to tie stance changes to performance changes, Benge's [swing mechanics and results changed drastically](https://ryanliam-bio.github.io/baseball_analytics/projects/stance-tracker/#benge-and-tatis) at the end of April. He was a legitimately great hitter from then on, earning a 130 wRC+ and a 4.4 fWAR thanks in part to a season-long top 20 OF Fielding Run Value. He (arguably) had a stronger analytical case than JJ, being less reliant on defensive value. He had a better wOBA/wRC+ than Sal Stewart. He had the oldhead 20/20 threshold and highlight-reel defensive assists. Yet he finished a distant second, only 7 first-place votes to Sal's 20.

Sal Stewart won convincingly, earning 20 of 30 possible first-place votes. No disrespect to the BBWAA members who voted for this award, but I really thought the baseball public had grown more analytically inclined in the last decade. He finished more than 2 WAR behind JJ and Benge, was brutal defensively, and was a worse hitter than Carson Benge. But he hit 32 home runs and drove in 112 runs.

None of this is meant to knock Sal Stewart the player or the person. Playing all 162 games as an above-average hitter at the age of 22 is impressive. Some of his defensive deficit can likely be attributed to his team situation forcing him to play primarily 1B instead of 3B, where he was about average in a limited sample. My frustration is that so many of the voters' decisions seemed to hinge on HRs and RBIs, the latter of which is arguably the most context-dependent offensive statistic out there. Even his homer total is inflated by one of the friendliest parks in baseball. We have not seen a ROY winner with this WAR deficit since 2010, and I'd argue those picks were easier to defend. This year's race feels much closer to 2007's Braun > Tulo.

Full disclaimer that I am obviously biased toward the analytical case here, but I was so wrong on this that I can't even be truly mad. If you asked me 3 months ago, I would've said that JJ's WAR lead was enough to keep him afloat until he broke out of his offensive slump. If you asked me 2 months ago, I would've said that Sal's September fade of a 56 wRC+ probably sank his case. If you asked me 1 month ago, after the regular season was fully wrapped up, I would've said that Benge ultimately gave voters a layup combination of traditional stats, analytical value, and splash plays. My read was never close, the market was right all along, and the analytical creep of awards voting is on hold for the time being.
